Comfort deserves a place in the conversation

Pain is information worth taking seriously. You do not need to push through it.

For adults 18+. Education, not a diagnosis or personalized care.

You can pause

If an activity hurts, stop or change it. You do not need to continue to protect someone’s feelings. “That hurts, and I want to pause” is enough.

Make room for appropriate care

Pain during sex can have different causes. ACOG recommends professional assessment for frequent or severe pain. A learning guide cannot identify a cause from a short description.

A recurring concern deserves attention even if you can sometimes avoid it. A clinician can discuss your symptoms and decide what assessment is appropriate.

Prepare a description

You might note where the discomfort is, when it began, what brings it on, and whether other symptoms occur. You can bring written notes and ask the clinician to explain each proposed step before you decide.

“I’m having pain during intimacy. I’d like help understanding the cause and my options.”

Keep choice in the room

You can choose other forms of closeness, or none at all. There is no obligation to replace an activity you stopped. A partner can help by listening, respecting the pause, and asking what support you would actually welcome.

This page does not evaluate urgency. If you think something is an emergency, use local emergency medical services.
