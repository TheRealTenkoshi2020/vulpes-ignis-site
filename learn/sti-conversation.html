The STI conversation can be an act of care

A practical conversation about testing, without turning it into a character judgment.

For adults 18+. Read to learn and prepare questions; a qualified professional can help with your own care.

Ask before the moment is urgent

You can bring up testing as part of an ordinary conversation about shared expectations: “Before we become sexually involved, I’d like to talk about testing and protection. What would help us both feel informed?”

Health information is not a measure of someone’s value. A respectful conversation makes room for questions and boundaries without humiliation.

Let testing answer testing questions

CDC notes that STIs may have no symptoms. Which tests someone needs, and how often, depends on their situation. A healthcare provider can discuss appropriate tests, timing, and which body sites should be tested.

A general “all clear” can leave important questions unanswered. Ask which infections were included and when testing happened. Testing has limits; a clinician can explain them.

Try practical language

“What testing have we each had, and when? Are there any results we’re waiting for? What protection do we want to use?”

You can set a boundary while you wait for information. If you are unsure what a result means, ask the testing service rather than trying to diagnose it together.

Find a starting point

The CDC testing guide explains options and links to testing services. You can take your questions to a local clinician or sexual-health clinic.

ONE SMALL THING TO TRY

Write one testing question you would want answered before a new intimate relationship. Practice saying it without apologizing for asking.
