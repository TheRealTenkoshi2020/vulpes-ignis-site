Clear limits make your yes easier to understand, too.

Name your boundary plainly

A boundary describes a limit you will honor. It can be about touch, topics, time, privacy, or how you are spoken to. You don’t have to supply a dramatic explanation for a reasonable limit.

“I’m happy to talk about this, but I’m not willing to be shouted at. If that happens, I’ll pause the conversation.”

Try describing what you are comfortable with, what you are not available for, and what you will do if the limit is crossed. Focus on actions you can choose.

Distinguish a request from control

You can ask another person to do something. You cannot make their choices for them. “I’d like us to discuss who sees our private messages” opens a conversation. Your own boundary might describe what information you are willing to share.

Relationships still involve agreements and consequences. The aim is to make those visible and freely understood.

Notice the response

A respectful response can include disappointment, questions, or a different preference. It should still leave room for your choice. Repeated pressure or retaliation can make a boundary harder to hold; seek support from someone you trust or a suitably qualified professional if you need it.

Allow revision

You can learn that a limit needs to change. You can move more slowly. You can say “I thought I was comfortable with that, but I’m not.” An earlier choice does not obligate you to repeat it.
