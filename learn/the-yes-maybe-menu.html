The Yes–Maybe–No Menu for Curious Couples

A conversation game with no score and no pressure.

For adults 18+. Education, not a diagnosis or personalized care.

A preference menu can turn vague curiosity into an easier conversation. It works best when both people understand that the menu is about interests, not obligations, and that even a yes is not permission for future activity.

A practical idea

Write ideas for nonsexual and romantic connection on separate cards: slow dancing, a shared bath, a massage, a new date spot, a long kiss, or a night of uninterrupted conversation. Sort each into yes, maybe, or no. Nobody has to explain a no.

What it might look like

One person marks massage as maybe because they dislike scented oils. The couple learns that an unscented hand massage might be appealing while a full-body massage is not.

Boundaries and wellbeing

Do not use the list as a negotiation ledger. A maybe means more discussion, not persuasion. Check in again if circumstances change, and never interpret silence as agreement.

A question to take with you

Which 'maybe' would be easiest to talk about without deciding whether to try it?

Neither person owes participation, disclosure, or physical intimacy. Preferences can change. Ask, listen, and make it easy to decline.

Build a menu worth discussing

Use ordinary and romantic possibilities together: cooking a new meal, slow dancing, a long kiss, a shared bath, trying a new restaurant, or a screen-free evening. Sorting them alongside one another reduces the impression that every conversation must end with sex. Each person can create their own menu before comparing answers.

What a maybe really means

Maybe can mean interested but unsure, curious only in theory, or interested under specific conditions. Ask which interpretation fits, but allow someone to say they do not know. A maybe never authorizes an activity and should not become a target for persuasion. When plans change, ask again rather than relying on an old list.

Turn overlap into an invitation

Find one idea both people independently marked yes. Discuss timing, comfort, privacy, and what would make it easy to stop. If there is no overlap today, choose a different way to connect. The purpose is learning about each other, not reaching a quota of shared preferences.

Small adjustments that matter

Include everyday affection alongside romantic activities so the conversation does not become a disguised sexual checklist. For each maybe, ask what information would help: privacy, timing, a description of the plan, or more time. It is also fine to leave a maybe unresolved indefinitely.
