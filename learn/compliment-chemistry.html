Compliments With Chemistry: Beyond 'You Look Good'

Specific attention can be more meaningful than grand declarations.

For adults 18+. Read to learn and prepare questions; a qualified professional can help with your own care.

On this page · 6 sections

Compliments often land best when they show that someone noticed a choice, effort, or quality. Generic praise is fine, but specificity gives a partner something real to feel seen for.

Try it together

Try naming a small action you appreciated, a creative decision you admired, or a moment when your partner made you laugh. Keep the focus on their experience rather than demanding a reaction.

An everyday example

A partner says, 'I loved how you made our guests feel welcome,' rather than repeating the same appearance-based compliment.

Consent and comfort

Avoid turning compliments into transactions or commenting on body features someone has said are sensitive. If praise makes a partner uncomfortable, ask what kind of acknowledgment feels better. Neither partner owes an explanation for declining or changing their mind.

A conversation starter

What is something about me you appreciate that I may not realize you notice?

Make it your own

Choose one part of the idea that fits your relationship and leave the rest. If you try it, check in afterward: did this feel enjoyable, neutral, or uncomfortable? Curiosity is useful even when the answer is 'not for us.' Respect privacy, accessibility needs, and differences in desire.

Putting the idea into practice

A three-part compliment experiment

Choose one compliment about effort, one about character, and one about a shared memory. For example, notice the care someone put into a meal, the patience they showed a friend, or how they made a difficult day lighter. Give each compliment when it is true, not as a batch of scripted lines.

Receiving praise can be complicated

Some people find direct compliments uncomfortable or suspect they are a prelude to a request. If your partner brushes praise aside, do not argue them into accepting it. Ask whether quiet appreciation, written notes, or practical gestures feel more natural.
