Make a little room

Choose ten minutes when everyone involved is willing and available. Put screens aside if that works for you. Agree that this check-in is a chance to understand each other, without an obligation to solve everything or become physically intimate afterward.

If today is a bad day, choose another time. Connection that requires someone to override their limits starts with the wrong bargain.

Three questions, slowly

  1. What has been taking up space for you lately?
  2. When did you feel connected to me this week?
  3. What is one small thing you would appreciate next week?

Take turns. The listener’s job is to hear the answer before explaining their own experience. “Thank you for telling me” is a perfectly useful response. So is “Can you help me understand that part?”

End with something possible

Choose an action small enough to fit your actual life. “Be more romantic” is hard to act on. “Let’s have coffee together on Saturday without our phones” is easier to understand.

If a larger conflict appears, name it and arrange a separate conversation. The check-in doesn’t have to become an emergency meeting with seventeen agenda items. It did not sign up for middle management.

You can check in with yourself, too

Ask what is weighing on you, what felt nourishing, and what you need more space for. Write a single sentence for each. You don’t need a partner to deserve care or to learn what matters to you.
