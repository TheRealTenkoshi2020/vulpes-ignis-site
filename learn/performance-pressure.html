Take the pressure off performance

A body response is not a verdict on your worth, your attraction, or your relationship.

For adults 18+. Education, not a diagnosis or personalized care.

Separate the event from its meaning

An experience can become more difficult when it is asked to prove love, attraction, masculinity, or competence. Try naming what happened without adding a verdict: “That wasn’t the response I expected.” You can decide together whether to pause, change the activity, or stop.

Leave room for a fuller picture

NIDDK describes erection difficulties as potentially involving health conditions, medicines, emotional factors, or lifestyle factors. A quiz cannot determine the cause. Persistent or worrying changes are worth discussing with a clinician.

Do not adjust a prescribed medicine based on a website. A prescriber can help you discuss possible effects and options.

Use words that reduce the test

“I like being close to you. I’m feeling pressure about how my body responds, and I’d like us to slow down.”

That sentence is an invitation to understand your experience. It does not require the other person to fix it. You can discuss closeness that feels welcome without making a particular response the price of admission.

Choose a useful next step

If the concern keeps returning, prepare a few notes about timing, changes, and what you want to ask. You deserve practical support without being reduced to a performance score.

ONE SMALL THING TO TRY

Name one thing that makes closeness feel less like an exam. If you have a persistent concern, add it to your questions for a clinician.
