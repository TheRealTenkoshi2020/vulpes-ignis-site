Your Intimacy Adventure Map: Build a Shared Bucket List

Create a flexible list of connection ideas with enthusiasm, boundaries, and room to change.

For adults 18+. Education, not a diagnosis or personalized care.

A shared list can be playful when it captures curiosity without creating obligations.

An idea to try

Make three columns: easy this week, someday maybe, and not for us. Include dates, conversations, travel, affection, and learning experiences rather than only sexual activities.

A realistic example

A couple adds dancing in the kitchen and visiting a new park to their list, then chooses the simplest idea first.

Consent and comfort

A bucket list is not a contract. Each activity needs fresh agreement, and either person can remove an item without debate.

Talk about it

What adventure would feel exciting even if nothing else happened afterward?

Make it yours

Choose only what feels enjoyable and manageable. An invitation can be declined or changed at any time, and no one owes physical intimacy in exchange for attention or effort. If you try the idea, ask what worked and what should change. A conversation that leads nowhere else can still be worthwhile.

Putting the idea into practice

Build a list with different scales

Add tiny ideas such as a new breakfast ritual, medium ideas such as a day trip, and someday ideas such as a class or vacation. Include a 'not interested' column so the list represents boundaries as well as enthusiasm.

Review without turning it into a contract

Interests can change. Revisit the map occasionally and remove ideas freely. When choosing an activity, ask for fresh agreement rather than assuming a past yes still applies. Shared anticipation should never become a source of pressure.

One more way to make it work

A useful map includes ideas at different scales: a five-minute ritual, an afternoon outing, and a long-term hope. Mark each with an estimated effort level rather than a deadline. Revisit the list when circumstances change. Removing an idea is as legitimate as adding one, and no entry should ever be treated as consent in advance.

A deeper dive

Three horizons

Sort ideas into this week, someday, and only a conversation. Include nonsexual experiences such as visiting a new park, trying a recipe, or learning a dance. A list can hold dreams without becoming a schedule. Revisit it when interests change and delete items freely.

Keep consent current

Writing an idea down is not permission to act on it later. Check again before each activity, and consider time, budget, health, privacy, and accessibility. If a plan stops being fun, change it. The adventure is discovering what suits your relationship, not finishing the list.
