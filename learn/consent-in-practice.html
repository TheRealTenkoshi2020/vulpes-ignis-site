Consent is a conversation that keeps going

A yes has meaning when a no is welcome. Here’s how to make room for both.

For adults 18+. Education, not a diagnosis or personalized care.

On this page · 4 sections

Keep choice at the center

Consent is a freely chosen agreement to a particular activity. It needs enough understanding to be meaningful, and it can change. A relationship, an earlier yes, a role label, or an agreement on paper does not grant permanent permission.

Someone needs the capacity to choose. Being asleep, unable to understand what is happening, or too impaired to make a choice is not a basis for consent. Pressure, threats, and fear can make a spoken agreement anything but free.

Ask clearly, then listen

“Would you like this?” is useful. So are “Do you want to continue?” and “Would you rather pause?” Avoid treating silence or lack of resistance as agreement. If you are unsure, stop and ask.

“You can change your mind. I’d rather know what feels right for you.”

Check-ins can be ordinary and warm. They don’t need to sound like a software license. What matters is whether the answer can be honest and whether it will be respected.

Roles don’t remove rights

Adult power-exchange or role-play agreements need explicit boundaries, a way to pause, and room to renegotiate. A role name does not override a person’s autonomy. Playful language should never create confusion about an actual stop.

Make changing your mind easy

If someone pauses or declines, stop the activity. You can talk later about feelings without turning the boundary into a debt. A no is not a request for persuasion. Consent matters as much after the tenth year together as it does at the beginning.

ONE SMALL THING TO TRY

Choose a simple check-in phrase that feels natural to you, such as “Keep going, change something, or pause?” Discuss it before you need it.
